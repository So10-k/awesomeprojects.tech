# Product

## Register

brand

The public website (home, search, name pages, directory, docs, stats, status, maintainers) is the primary surface and is brand. My domains and the admin dashboard at admin.awesomeprojects.tech are product surfaces: treat them with the product register.

## Users

- **Developers and builders** (students, hobbyists, open-source people) who just made something and want a free, memorable name for it: `alice.awesomeprojects.tech`. They arrive from a README, a friend, or a search. They're usually on a laptop with a dark editor open, mid-flow, and they want to claim a name in under a minute without an account form.
- **Name owners** coming back to manage records, redeploy a hosted site, or add a collaborator (My domains).
- **Maintainers** (trainee → maintainer → lead → owner) running the registry: reviewing requests, handling abuse, on duty, often in the evening. They live in the admin dashboard and GitHub.

## Product Purpose

awesomeprojects.tech is a free subdomain registry in the spirit of is-a.dev and eu.org: every name is a pull request, reviewed by a bot and people, recorded in public, with free hosting on our own GitHub server for those who want it. Success: a builder goes from "I want a name" to a live name fast and trusts us enough to point real projects at it; maintainers can run it calmly at scale; it feels like a real piece of internet infrastructure that happens to be fun.

## Brand Personality

**Precise infrastructure, with a playful hacker underneath.**
- Precise: exact, calm, quietly confident. Every word and pixel earns its place, like a well-run nameserver. Linear/Vercel-grade control of type and spacing.
- Playful: clever details that reward poking around (a live zone record, a `whois` that answers, keyboard shortcuts everywhere, small jokes for developers). Never at the cost of clarity.
- Voice: short, plain, second person, a wink allowed. "Free names for the things you build." Not hype, not corporate.

## Anti-references

- **Hacker-terminal cliché**: green-on-black monospace everything, fake matrix rain, glitch effects, "hacking" costume.
- **The old neon look**: the mint → blue → pink gradient on navy with a grid (being retired).
- **Corporate registrars** (GoDaddy, Namecheap): upsells, banners, coupon energy, cluttered checkout.
- **Generic SaaS landing**: gradient blob hero, three identical feature cards, logo wall, pricing table.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what we host.** The site is fast, static, accessible and honest, the way we ask hosted sites to be.
2. **Show the real thing.** Real names, real records, real deploys, real numbers from the registry instead of illustrations of them.
3. **Precision first, play second.** Play lives in details and interactions, never in the way of reading or claiming a name.
4. **Public by default.** Every decision is visible (PRs, the activity log, transparency numbers), and the design makes that openness feel like a feature.
5. **Keyboard is a first-class citizen.** Everything a developer or maintainer does has a fast path.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: body text ≥4.5:1, visible focus everywhere, full keyboard use, semantic landmarks, `prefers-reduced-motion` honored on every animation (crossfade or none), color never the only signal (status always has text or an icon too).
